Every course awarded with honours names one of four methods in its handbook entry
(e.g. 1862: "Honours Grade is calculated using Honours Grade Method 2"). The
method sets how each subject is weighted in the honours WAM and the WAM needed
for each class.

## Method 2 (used by the Bachelor of Engineering (Honours), including 1862)

Weighting by subject level: **4** for 400-level, **3** for 300-level, **2** for
200-level, **1** for 100-level.

| Class | Honours WAM |
|---|---|
| Class I | 77.5% to 100% |
| Class II, Division 1 | 72.5% to less than 77.5% |
| Class II, Division 2 | 67.5% to less than 72.5% |
| Class III | 62.5% to less than 67.5% |

**1862 exception:** Honours Class III is **not awarded** for 1862. A student below
67.5% still graduates *with Honours*, but no grade of Honours is awarded.

Honours WAM (Method 2) = Σ(mark × CP × level weight) ÷ Σ(CP × level weight).

## Other methods (for comparison)

| Method | Weighting (400 / 300 / 200 / 100) | Class I | Class II Div 1 | Class II Div 2 | Class III |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 / 0 / 0 / 0 (400-level and Honours-level only) | 85–100 | 75–<85 | 65–<75 | 50–<65 |
| 3 | 4 / 1 / 0 / 0 | 80–100 | 72.5–<80 | 65–<72.5 | — |
| 4 | 1 / 1 / 1 / 1 | 75–100 | 72.5–<75 | 70–<72.5 | — |

## Honours after a Bachelor of Computer Science

Students in the Bachelor of Computer Science who satisfy entry requirements may
be eligible to apply for the Bachelor of Computer Science (Honours). Contact the
Academic Program Director for entry requirements — do not state a WAM cutoff
for this; it is not published in the handbook.
